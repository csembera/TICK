Outreach to whoever fills the Chief AI Officer seat8

outreach

The Chief AI Officer req came off the board after 154 days and has not been reposted.

What the seat was hired for“Requirements: 10+ years leading applied AI programs, including an enterprise rollout of generative AI and moving demand forecasting off spreadsheets onto production models; experience in distribution or manufacturing.”2
  1. Confirm the seat is filled before anything goes out. A req leaving the board usually means a hire, but it can also mean a pause or a rewrite. TICK sees the posting, not who took it, so check the company’s newsroom for an announcement before you write to anyone.
  2. Find who owned AI before the seat filled. The AI terms hit the board in April, roughly five months before the req closed, so the program predates the new officer. They either adopt it or redirect it, and those are two different conversations. Whoever ran it is also your alternate entry if the new officer proves unreachable.
  3. Write to the job the seat was hired for. The req asked for an enterprise generative AI rollout and forecasting moved onto production models2. That is the mandate the new officer arrives with. Speak to it, and a cold note to a new executive reads as relevant rather than generic.
  4. Lead with the sequencing risk. Skip the category education: the seat asked for distribution experience, so the new officer knows the category. What they can’t see yet from the inside is how the migration wave and the AI agenda collide for the same platform capacity. That’s the opening line worth having.
  5. Open a second thread through the migration. Single-threading a cold account through one executive is fragile. The Cloud Migration Program Manager5 and Cloud Network Engineer4 reqs point to an infrastructure leader with committed budget and a deadline this quarter. That person has a problem now; the new officer has a plan. Work both.
  6. Move inside the quarter. The evangelist req6 under that seat is open and the platform hires haven’t landed. Mandate without capacity is the condition you’re selling into, and it closes once the team is staffed.

Workshop and thought leadership built from the postings9

pitch

AI and machine learning now reaches 6 open roles, and machine learning, generative ai, llm, and bedrock only appeared here in April.

In their words“You will evaluate LLM platforms including AWS Bedrock and Vertex AI and define the reference architecture teams build against.”10
  1. The centerpiece: a reference architecture working session. Their own req10 says teams are evaluating Bedrock and Vertex and that the reference architecture is undefined. That is a decision in progress with no incumbent answer, and it's the highest-leverage thing on this board. A half-day working session on the platform decision — selection criteria, what gets locked in, what stays reversible — puts us in the room while it's being made. Once it's written, everything after is integration against someone else's choices.
  2. The thesis to publish: streaming forecasting on a lakehouse. Kafka is the only new term this week and it landed in the Databricks req3. The Chief AI Officer req asked for forecasting moved onto production models2. Those are the same architecture. A written point of view on real-time forecasting for distribution — what the ingestion layer has to do, where latency actually matters, what breaks at their scale — speaks to that seat’s first mandate and their new plumbing at once.
  3. The question that shapes the pitch: why Vertex. AWS appears in 15 of 39 postings, so naming Vertex alongside Bedrock is deliberate. Either there's real GCP in the estate or someone wants pricing leverage. Ask early, because the answer changes whether we're proposing a single-platform reference architecture or a portability layer.
  4. The proof to bring. One comparable estate that made this exact platform call, including what it cost them where they got it wrong. An evaluation already underway is moved by specifics from someone who's done it, not by capability material.
  5. The secondary session: migration and AI capacity collision. Network4, landing zone, data platform3, and program management5 are all being staffed simultaneously while six AI roles sit open. Both programs want the same people. A short session on sequencing those two workstreams is credible with the infrastructure leader and with the new officer, which is what makes it a useful second thread.
